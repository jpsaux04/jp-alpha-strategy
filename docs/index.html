Against the beta-matched passive book — the hurdle that holds market exposure equal, described below — the excess is +6.35%. That is the arithmetic of this window and it is not in dispute: on this sample the book outperformed both passive alternatives, gross of costs.

What that does not yet establish is skill. The live alpha carries t = +1.27 on n = 85 daily observations; |t| > 1.96 is the conventional bar. At this sample size a strategy with genuinely no edge produces a result this good or better a meaningful fraction of the time, so the excess above is consistent with skill without being evidence of it. The multi-year backtest points the other way — SPY wins there — and a three-month window in which this book is ahead does not overturn it. The items below are why the question is still open, not a claim that the number above is wrong.

  • No statistically significant alpha has been demonstrated. Factor attribution (Phase 8, 3/4/6-factor and CAPM) finds zero significant alpha in any configuration. The long-only candidate measured there (V4, 2.0×ATR) shows alpha +2.26%/yr with t = 0.57 (95% CI −5.44% to +9.96%) — statistically indistinguishable from zero. The previously live long/short V3 showed alpha −10.77%/yr, t = −1.91. Measured beta is ~0.55: the return is market exposure, not skill. The deployed V5 (1.5×ATR) does not change this — its alpha against the strategy's own universe is +1.51%/yr (t = +0.38). V5 was deployed to make realised risk equal the 1% the system claims to risk, not to create an edge.
  • The correct benchmark is not 0%. SPY buy & hold returned +229% (Sharpe 0.93) over 2019–2026, beating every strategy variant tested. A passive 0.55×SPY + 0.45 cash book reproduces essentially the whole result. Both benchmarks are plotted on the equity chart above.

Earlier versions of this dashboard and older documents reported a 65.4% / 85.4% win rate; those were fragment-level and upward-biased, because tiered exits emit up to three exit fragments per position and the T1 fragment is a win by construction. Trade counts were inflated the same way (e.g. 1,279 backtest “trades” were really 688 positions). Fragment counts are still shown, but labelled as fragments.
  • The live track record is short. 86 equity observations (2026-05-13 → 2026-10-15) and 34 closed positions — far too few to distinguish skill from luck. Ratios such as Sharpe/Sortino/Calmar over this window are descriptive only, carry no inferential weight, and should not be extrapolated.
